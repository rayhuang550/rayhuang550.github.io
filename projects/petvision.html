PetVision: AI-Powered Multi-Pet Feeder

Notes

A pet feeder that recognizes which pet is at the bowl and gives it the right food on its own schedule. I designed and built the hardware, firmware, vision service and companion app. It won first place in the 2026 IEEE SSCS Arduino Contest.

Area

Embedded, computer vision

Stack

ESP32-S3, C++, FreeRTOS, YOLO, DINOv2, React, Firebase

How it works

The feeder runs on an ESP32-S3. When a pet walks up, the camera takes a photo and sends it to a vision service on a computer on the same network. The service finds the pet with YOLO, crops it, and turns the crop into a DINOv2 embedding. The ESP32 compares that embedding with the saved reference photos of each pet to decide who is at the bowl.

The firmware is C++ on FreeRTOS, with network, command and control tasks split across both cores. It reads a 2 kg load cell through a NAU7802 over I2C and an Arducam camera over SPI, and drives a 28BYJ-48 stepper that turns the dispensing drum.

Dispensing is closed loop. A 50 Hz controller pulses the stepper until the filtered load-cell reading reaches that pet's target weight. Before feeding, it checks that the bowl is empty and that the pet hasn't reached its daily limit.

A React web app connected through Firebase is used to add pets, set portions and schedules, and see each pet's feeding history. The dispenser and load-cell mount are 3D printed.

Recognition test

I gave the system three reference photos of each of two cats, then ran it on 46 other photos of them. Every photo was matched to the right cat. YOLO didn't find the cat in two of them, and one of those scored below the confidence threshold.
